You are RETOUCHING real product cut-outs into one scene photograph.
You are NOT creating new photos of the products. Every product pixel already exists — reuse it.

INPUT IMAGES (fixed slots):

  * Some of these slots may be empty. The requirement card states how many pieces this run has —

- The LAST input image is the colour reference (present only when the requirement card says so).

  * When the requirement card says there is no colour reference, every image you receive is a

★★ DO NOT REDRAW THE PRODUCTS — HARD REQUIREMENT.
Preserve, pixel for pixel wherever possible: each garment's shape, cut, silhouette, outline, its
flat-laid form, every seam, every elastic binding, the leg-opening turn-back, the gusset panel, the
fabric's knit texture, and the printed logo with every character intact.

★★ WHAT YOU MAY DO WITH THE PIECES — TWO-DIMENSIONAL PLACEMENT ONLY.
Allowed, and encouraged so the composition looks styled rather than catalogued:
  - move each piece anywhere in the frame (translation)
  - scale each piece freely — the near one clearly larger, the far ones clearly smaller
  - offset them diagonally / stagger them instead of lining them up in a row
  - let edges overlap, one piece resting partly over another
  - leave breathing space around the group; do not fill the frame edge to edge
Forbidden, every one of these means recomputing a new three-dimensional form, which is redrawing:
  - do NOT fold a piece, do NOT stack folded pieces
  - do NOT stand a piece up, prop it up, or hang it
  - do NOT roll or curl a piece
  - do NOT turn a piece to a side or three-quarter angle
  - do NOT drape a piece over a prop, a hand, or an edge
  - do NOT re-pose, re-arrange or re-fold the cloth in any way
Every piece stays laid out flat, in exactly the shape it has in its input image. The only thing that
changes about its outline is the perspective foreshortening described under CAMERA below.

★★ CAMERA — HIGH OBLIQUE, ABOUT 60-70° DOWN FROM HORIZONTAL. NOT straight down, NOT eye level.
Look down onto the table steeply, but with enough tilt that the table surface recedes into the frame
and the scene has real depth. Concretely:
  - The table surface runs from the bottom of the frame back into the upper third; its far edge and
    the background are visible up there.
  - A round tray or plate reads as an ELLIPSE, its foreshortening consistent with the camera tilt.
    Every object in the frame shares that one consistent perspective.
  - The camera angle is fixed HERE. If the requirement card describes some other angle, ignore that
    part of it; take only the surface, light and props from the card.

★★ HOW THE FLAT PIECES SIT UNDER THAT CAMERA — READ THIS CAREFULLY.
Each input is a cut-out photographed straight down, so it has no perspective of its own. Lay each
piece FLAT on the table surface and render it as that same flat piece seen obliquely. What that
means, exactly:
  - Allowed: projective foreshortening — the piece gets compressed along the viewing direction the
    way any flat object does when you tilt the camera. This is a perspective transform of a flat
    shape, nothing more.
  - Forbidden: using the tilt as licence to redraw. The seams, the elastic binding and its width
    relative to the body, the gusset outline, the waistband proportion and the printed logo are all
    properties of the flat piece — after foreshortening they must still be recognisably the same
    piece, just viewed at an angle.
  - The pieces lie ON the table plane, flat. They are never lifted, tilted up, leaned, propped,
    floated, or given a three-dimensional puffed-up body.
  - Every piece shares the table plane and therefore the same foreshortening. Do not give one piece
    a different viewing angle from another.

★★ COMPOSITION — DEPTH, NOT A ROW.
Build an arrangement that recedes, the way a styled editorial flat-lay does:
  - ONE hero piece nearest the camera, largest, sitting low in the frame and taking up a good share
    of it. It is the one the eye lands on first.
  - The remaining pieces sit further back, progressively smaller with distance, staggered diagonally
    rather than lined up, each offset from the next, edges overlapping a little.
  - Leave breathing space; do not fill the frame edge to edge, and do not centre everything.

★★ DEPTH OF FIELD — GRADED, NOT UNIFORM.
The hero piece in front is tack sharp: every seam, binding edge and logo character crisp. The pieces
further back are very slightly softer. The props and the far table are softest. This gradient is what
separates a photograph from a paste-up — but never blur a product to the point its seams disappear.

★★ THE SURFACE MUST BE DARKER THAN THE PRODUCTS.
These are pale garments. A pale surface swallows them. Whatever surface the requirement card names,
render it clearly deeper in value than the products so the pieces are pushed forward by contrast.
Never place pale products on a near-white or equally pale surface.

★★ LIGHTING — ONE DIRECTIONAL SOFT LIGHT. FLAT LIGHT IS FORBIDDEN.
A single soft key light coming from one side, large and diffused, giving a readable gradient across
the surface and soft directional shading on the fabric folds. Do NOT light the frame evenly from the
front or from directly above; even, shadowless light is what makes a flat-lay look cheap and dated.
Fill the shadow side gently, never to the point of erasing the direction.

★★ CONTACT SHADOWS — MANDATORY.
Each piece casts its own soft contact shadow onto the surface, darkest and tightest where the cloth
touches, softening outward, all shadows falling in the same direction as the key light. Without this
the products float. Shadows land on the surface only; they never darken a neighbouring piece's
colour.

★ THE SCENE IS CHOSEN UPSTREAM, NOT BY THIS PROMPT.

  `SCENE (use this verbatim):`. Build exactly that scene — its surface, its props, its light quality.

  Do NOT default to beige linen with dried flowers and ceramic dishes — that is one option among
  many, not the house style. Different runs are meant to look genuinely different.
  Only if no scene block reaches you at all: use a plain deep-neutral surface, soft directional side

  The scene block never overrides the camera, the composition-depth rule, the
  surface-darker-than-product rule, the lighting rule or the contact-shadow rule — those are fixed

★ PROPS CARRY NO REAL BRANDS. Any bottle, tube, jar, book, keyboard or packaging used as a prop must

★★ NO GRAPHIC ANNOTATION OF ANY KIND. Do not draw arrows, dashed lines, callout leaders, measurement

★★ THE COLOURWAYS MUST STAY DISTINCT. Every piece keeps its own colour. Never make two pieces the
